The model: money goes straight to the people

We built HHH because we watched too much money stop in the machine on its way to the people it was meant for. Our model is direct. The foundation pays for housing, food, power, tools, and training itself, straight at the source. Nobody in our community splits cash, and nobody waits on a middleman to decide their worth.

People earn real wages through real foundation jobs. Our crews clean up trash and crime corridors around town, fix up houses, run our vending lanes, and build the very community they live in. Everybody gets the basics. The people who pull weight build the fuller share, and they can point at what their hands made.

Land, the honest way

Our land engine is the Arkansas Commissioner of State Lands post auction route, and it works in plain steps.

  1. An owner stops paying property taxes, and after the legal waiting period the county certifies the parcel to the Commissioner of State Lands.
  2. The state offers the parcel at a public auction, starting at the taxes, interest, penalties, and costs owed.
  3. Parcels nobody buys at auction move about 30 days later onto the state post auction list online, where a few hundred dollars can buy a piece of land outright.
  4. The buyer receives a Limited Warranty Deed. The former owner holds a short redemption window, and a litigation period follows, so we research every parcel first and start no major work until the window closes.

One rule never bends. Parcels carrying property owners association or homeowners association dues are out on sight. Those dues run forever and would bleed the community dry. We target land with water access and freedom of use.

Two more lanes grow the map. Owners buried in back taxes and code penalties can convey property directly, and families can give land and estate gifts as a legacy. Abandoned houses, the bandos, get fixed by our own crews and turned into homes and rentals, and every house we fix lifts the street around it.

Work lanes that fund the mission

Hot dog carts. Our carts work the convention center and downtown crowds, and food vending is one of our first earned income lanes.
Jewelry and crafts. Our makers build crystal jewelry, candles, and upcycled crafts and woodwork, sold in our own shop lanes.
HHH clothing. Our shirts carry our slogan, Love God, love people, and every shirt walking around town tells our story and points people back to this site.
Camo Santa. At the holidays, our volunteers ring the red kettles in camouflage Santa costumes, and Camo Santa raises money for HHH and for veterans.

Sister chapters: our vision

This part is vision, and we label it as vision. Hot Springs is our home base and our main clubhouse. From here we grow encampments and sister chapters around Arkansas, the way a motorcycle club grows chapters, so people have real choices about where to land. Once the model is proven here and the story travels, we believe chapters follow across the country. Far out on the horizon sits a self sufficient community on private land, built and run by the people who live there.
